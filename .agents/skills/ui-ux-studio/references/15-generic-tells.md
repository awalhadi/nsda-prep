# 15. Generic tells: what reads as templated or AI-made

Generated design today clusters around a few looks. Each one is legitimate for some briefs, but
they are **defaults rather than choices**, and they show up whatever the subject. Where the brief
pins down a look, follow it exactly, including when it asks for one of these. Where the brief leaves
an axis free, do not spend that freedom on a default.

## The five clusters

| # | The look | Legitimate when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm cream background (near #F4F1EA), high-contrast serif display, terracotta or clay accent (near #D97757, which is also Anthropic's own Claude accent, so on a client's brief it reads as a tell) | Print-heritage, food or craft brands that really have that material history |
| 2 | Near-black background with one bright acid-green or vermilion accent | Developer tools or nightlife where the audience lives in dark interfaces, and the accent means one thing |
| 3 | Broadsheet layout: hairline rules, zero radius, dense newspaper columns | Real editorial products with long-form reading and many stories |
| 4 | The SaaS card kit: everything in identical rounded cards, one radius on everything, the same soft grey shadow (`rgba(0,0,0,.1)`) under each, gradient washes as decoration | Dashboards where items really are equal, comparable units |
| 5 | Template chrome: a tracked-out ALL-CAPS eyebrow above every heading; meta joined with middle dots (`A · B · C`); labels built as `WORD — fragment`; tinted near-black (#0B0B0B, #111) standing in for black; monospace for small data labels; `→` appended to link and button text | Each item alone can be right once; the set together, everywhere, is the tell |

**Reading "legitimate when".** Judge the *client's* product, not the world of the client's
customers. Software for bakeries is not a craft brand, even though its users bake; a bakery's own
site may be. And when items really are comparable (pricing tiers, plans), keep the comparison (a
table, aligned rows) but drop the kit's decoration: identical radius, soft shadow, gradient wash.

## Typographic tells

- Accenting one word or phrase in a headline (italic, bold or a different colour).
- All caps for labels and eyebrows.
- Labels above content that add nothing ("Overview", "Features", "Why us").
- A big number + small label + gradient accent hero, when the number is not the story.

## Structural tells

- Numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03) on content that is not a sequence.
- Borders, dividers and outlines that do not separate anything meaningful.
- Every section the same shape: eyebrow, heading, one line, a three-card grid.

## Motion tells

- Fade-and-slide-up entrances on every section.
- Hover lift and shadow on every card.
- Scattered small effects instead of one orchestrated moment (see 10).
- Gradients that drift, shine or pulse on their own with no message.

## Copy tells

- Clever headlines that say nothing specific ("Unlock your potential").
- Sales verbs on plain things ("Supercharge your settings").
- Vague CTAs ("Get started", "Learn more") where the action could be named.
- Generic placeholder content instead of the brief's real subject matter (see 12 and 13).

## The genericity test (run on every direction plan)

1. For each axis (colour, type, layout, hero, motion, copy), ask: *if I received a similar brief
   for a different product, would I arrive at the same answer?*
2. If yes, the choice is a default. Either justify it from the brief in one sentence, or change it.
3. Check the plan against the five clusters and the lists above. Count the tells; more than one
   unjustified tell means the plan is revised before anything is built.
4. Write the result into the plan: what changed and why.

Example: "Colour was cream + terracotta (cluster 1). The subject is a Bangla exam on cheap phones
at night, which has no print-heritage reason. Changed to a low-glare cool paper tone with the brand
indigo as the only accent; contrast measured."

## In a critique

A generic tell is a valid *Problem* row only with a reason: it does not fit the subject, it
competes with the one bold move, or it carries no information. "It looks AI-made" alone is not
evidence; name the tell and its cost.
